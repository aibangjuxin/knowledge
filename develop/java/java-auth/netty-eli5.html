Netty is a smart post office for computer messages.

When programs on different computers want to talk, Netty helps them do it quickly.

The trigger: two programs want to talk

Your app and another computer. They speak bytes — long rows of 0s and 1s. They need a helper to package, send, unpack, and reply — without your app doing the boring parts.

The middle: a chain of small helpers

Bytes go through a pipeline — a row of small helpers. Helper 1 checks security. Helper 2 unzips the message. Helper 3 turns bytes into your app's data type. Each helper does one tiny job.

The middle: one worker per conversation

An EventLoop is a worker who watches many conversations. Each conversation stays with the same worker — messages never get jumbled. One worker handles thousands of chats at once.

The middle: speaks many languages

Netty already knows how to chat in HTTP, WebSocket, gRPC (a way for programs to call each other), and more. You pick which language. You do not rewrite the rules.

The outcome: fast, clean, safe

Your app talks to thousands of computers at once — quickly, without jumbled messages. Netty is the post office that never sleeps.

Who controls it: the open-source community

Netty is free, open-source software. The Netty project — run by volunteer maintainers — decides what it does. Anyone can read its code, propose changes, or report problems (called CVEs) when they find a security flaw.
