How does GCP Private Service Connect work?

Imagine two separate buildings. PSC is a private hallway between them — no public street needed.

The trigger

Your app needs to reach a managed service (Cloud SQL, Memorystore, a partner API) — and stay on Google's private wires, never the public internet.

The middle

Forward: The producer publishes a Service Attachment — a labeled front door. You make a PSC NEG (Network Endpoint Group — a private mailbox) in your VPC; a private IP from your subnet is its address. Traffic stays on Google's backbone.

The reverse direction

Who set this up? The producer's Service Attachment has an accept list — only listed projects or service accounts get in. Every connection is logged in VPC Flow Logs for audit.

The outcome

Result: a private, approved, audited path between two VPCs. No public IPs, no peering, no shared CIDR. The producer's network stays invisible — only the single service is exposed.

Who controls it

Setup & ownership Producer creates the Service Attachment and curates the accept list. Consumer creates the PSC NEG and binds it to a backend. Both sides use IAM (Google's permission system) to grant or revoke access. All traffic appears in VPC Flow Logs.
